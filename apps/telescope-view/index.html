Your eye

Young eyes open to about 7 mm in the dark; by 60 most open to about 5 mm.

Screen

At 1× the eyepiece’s field fills the view. Raise it to inspect detail your eye would see at that size.

Every view is computed from wave optics: the light through your aperture, obstruction and spider is Fourier-transformed at seven wavelengths into a diffraction pattern, bent by a drifting turbulent air layer and the refractor’s focus error at each colour, then blurred into the target. Brightness is then passed through a model of the dark-adapted eye: surface brightness through the exit pupil, sky glow, colour fading as rods take over, coarser vision in dim light, and a detection threshold that hides what is too faint. The planets, Moon and deep-sky objects are drawn procedurally with real sizes and brightness, so their details are plausible, not a map. Jupiter’s moons are placed for the date shown.
