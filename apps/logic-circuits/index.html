Save and share

Your tabs and chips are saved in this browser as you go. Export saves the open tab's board with all your chips, to back them up or give them to someone else. Importing opens the file's board in a new tab.

How it works
  • Wire by dragging from one pin to another. Outputs sit on the right of a part, inputs on the left. An output can feed many inputs; each input takes one wire. While wiring, click empty space to add bends.
  • Move parts by dragging them. Drag empty space to select an area with a mouse, or to move the board on a touch screen. Scroll or pinch to zoom; drag with the right mouse button or hold Space to move the board.
  • Make a chip by selecting a finished circuit and pressing Make chip. Switches and buttons turn into input pins, lights into output pins. Pins start out ordered column by column, top to bottom, as they sit on the board; move them up or down in the Make chip window, or later with Details under My chips. Their labels become the pin names.
  • Folders keep My chips tidy. Press New folder, then drag a chip onto a folder, or pick its folder in the Make chip window or in Details. Click a folder's name to fold it shut. Deleting a folder keeps its chips.
  • Buses carry many bits on one thick wire, with the number it holds written on it. Set a switch or light to 2 to 16 bits to send or show a whole number; click a bit to flip it. A Bus join bundles single wires into a bus and a Bus split opens one up, top pin first as bit 0. Inside a chip, a multi-bit switch or light becomes one bus pin, drawn square. A bus only connects to a pin of the same width.
  • Tabs hold separate boards that share your chips. Halfway through a build and need a new chip? Press + above the board, build and make the chip there, then switch back. Click the open tab's name to rename it.
  • Chips inside chips: a chip is a part like any other, so you can use it to build bigger chips. Double-click a chip to open it and change it; every copy updates.
  • Timing: each gate reacts one tick after its inputs change, and the board runs ticks until nothing changes. Loops that never settle, like a NOT gate wired to itself, keep flickering.

A path to a computer:

  1. Build NOT, AND and OR out of NAND gates alone.
  2. Half adder (XOR and AND), then a full adder from two half adders, then a 4-bit adder.
  3. An SR latch from two NOR gates, then a D latch and a D flip-flop that changes on the clock.
  4. A 4-bit register and a counter, with a Hex display to watch it count.
  5. Multiplexers, an ALU, memory, and a program counter: that is a CPU.

Shortcuts: Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V copy and paste, Ctrl+D duplicate, Ctrl+A select all, Del delete, F fit, Esc cancel.
