Mission clock 0:00:00 Thrusters Warp left

A units bug in the navigation software sent humanity's first crewed warp ship 63,000 times too far, out past the Milky Way. Find the Sun and click it. Warp jumps are blind and burn fuel you cannot refill. The mission clock never stops; jumps and exposures add their length and every wrong claim costs an hour.

Warp drive

Jumps go along the ship's nose, the middle of the big front window, and land up to 2% of the distance off target. As the bubble forms, the sky squeezes ahead of you and turns blue until the microwave background glows white-hot; blast shutters close against it and open again as you drop out. The drive is stuck at the bugged speed, about 1,500 ly/s, and burns fuel for every second of warp. You have 4:30 of warp, 1.5× the trip home, so plan before you jump. Thrusters are free but top out at 10 ly/s.

Camera

Wide-field camera with a 36 mm lens, framed like your eyes. It stacks 10 s frames; each takes 1 s of real time and adds 10 s to the clock. Your ship holds still until the exposure ends or you stop it.

Stars are procedural, except about 7,800 naked-eye stars within 3,000 light-years from Hipparcos. Nebulae: the 50 most ionizing star-forming complexes in Russeil (2003) plus Orion, Eagle, Lagoon, Rosette, North America and California, with rough distances and brightness. Spiral arms follow Reid et al. (2019). Everything you see is where it was when its light left, and the galaxy turns while that light travels. The warp view comes from light rays traced through an Alcubierre bubble 100 m across with a 10 m wall. The cutscene music is Koto garden from Song Studio, sped up from 72 to 200 bpm.
