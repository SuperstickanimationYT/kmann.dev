How it works

1D rules use Wolfram's numbering. Each cell looks at itself and its two neighbours, giving 8 cases from 111 down to 000; write the new state for each case as a binary number and that is the rule. Rule 110 is 01101110. Each new generation is drawn under the last one, so time runs down the screen.

2D rules use B/S notation. A dead cell is born with any count of live neighbours listed after B, and a live cell survives with any count listed after S. Conway's Life is B3/S23. Neighbours are the 8 cells around a cell.

Infinite boards have no edge. Outside the busy area the board is the background repeated forever, so a glider can travel as far as it likes.

An ether is a background that repeats in both space and time. Gliders in Rule 110 only exist inside one. The search tries every repeating strip up to 16 cells wide (1D) or tile up to 16 cells in area (2D) and keeps the ones that come back to themselves. Then it runs a random board and measures how much of it settles into each one, and lists the most common first. A rule whose random boards settle into an ether is one where gliders on that ether are likely to show up by themselves.

With Dim the background on, any cell that fits the chosen background pattern is drawn faintly, and everything else is bright.

Select drags out a box (in 1D, a stretch of the newest row). Copy, cut or clear it, then Paste and click to place copies. Copying also puts the cells on your system clipboard as RLE, the text format Golly and LifeWiki use, and you can paste RLE from there too.

The Rule 110 gliders are found by the same kind of search: flip a few cells of the ether and keep the ones that turn into a single object that repeats as it moves.

Keys: Space play/pause, N step, F fit, Ctrl+C / Ctrl+X / Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste, Del clear, R rotate, H flip, Esc cancel.
