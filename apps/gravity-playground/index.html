Gravity Playground

Spacetime

General relativity with 2 space dimensions. Masses don't pull: each one cuts a wedge out of space (hatched) and glues the edges together, turning flat space into a cone. Balls fly straight; one that reaches a wedge's edge carries on from the opposite edge. Masses stay put.

Where the wedge is cut from is a drawing choice, like where you cut a paper cone: every direction gives the same cone.

Force law

In a truly 2D universe gravity would fall off as 1/r. Real 3D gravity is 1/r². Only 1/r² (and r) give closed elliptical orbits; others make orbits precess into rosettes, and 1/r³ or steeper can't hold a stable orbit at all.

MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics) makes gravity stronger wherever it is weak, which speeds up outer orbits in galaxies without dark matter. Here it boosts each body's total pull, an approximation that doesn't conserve momentum exactly. Reload a galaxy after switching so its orbits match.

Every pair of bodies also repels, harder the farther apart they are. Summed over all pairs, that is one outward push from the centre of mass that grows with distance: the Newtonian form of a cosmological constant.

New body

Each pixel you drag adds 0.5 km/s, measured from whatever pulls hardest where you start, so zoom out to throw something in from far away.

Controls

Mouse

  • Drag empty space: pull back like a slingshot to launch a planet the opposite way; the arrow is its velocity and the dashed line its path.
  • Click empty space: drop a planet.
  • Drag a body: grab and throw it. Click one: select it.
  • Right-click a body: delete it. Right- or middle-drag: pan. Wheel: zoom.
  • Keys: Space pause, Delete remove selected, T trails, G glow, F fit view, Esc deselect.

Real star systems

  • The "Real star systems, to scale" scenes use real masses, sizes and distances. Planets start where they really are today, flattened onto one plane. The status line shows the simulated date and the scale bar shows distance.
  • Throw in a rogue planet, a star or a black hole, then speed time up to watch the flyby. Select a body and press Hold still to watch everything from its point of view: it stays in the middle of the screen, and trails show motion relative to it. Hold the Sun still to see clean heliocentric orbits even while a flyby drags the Sun away.
  • Close passes get smaller time steps automatically, so a black hole can graze a planet without the orbits blowing up.
  • It's still Newtonian gravity in 2D: no relativity, no tidal disruption. Black holes swallow whatever touches their event horizon, which is only tens of km wide.

Galaxies

  • Stars (white specks) and dark matter (faint violet) pass through each other instead of merging, and can't be grabbed.
  • Compare "Galaxy" with "Galaxy without dark matter": without a halo the disk forms a bar and flings its outskirts away. Tick MOND and reload the bare galaxy to see whether MOND holds it together instead.

Star formation

  • "Collapsing gas cloud" is gas (orange) with pressure and friction. Dense gas becomes a star, which swallows gas it touches; leftover gas that spins around the star forms a disk.
  • Gas on a near-circular orbit for 1.5 s condenses into a solid planetesimal. Planetesimals merge, and once one passes mass 5 it pulls in gas and grows into a giant. Every cloud is random: expect single stars, binaries, migrating or ejected giants.

Cosmic web

  • A patch of young universe: dark matter (violet) and gas (orange) flying apart with the expansion, seeded with faint ripples. Gravity slows the expansion, the ripples grow into filaments, and halos form where filaments meet. Gas that piles up dense enough in a halo turns into stars, which glow white as galaxies. "space ×" in the status line is how much the patch has grown.
  • The view zooms out with the expansion, so clusters stay put on screen. Untick "Zoom out with the expansion" to watch the bubble grow instead.
  • Loading it switches gravity to 1/r and turns dark energy on. With 1/r a uniform disc pulls inward in proportion to distance, just as dark energy pushes out, so the whole patch expands evenly.
  • In 2D, 1/r gravity binds everything: without dark energy the expansion always stops and the patch falls back together. Set dark energy to off right after loading to watch it.

2+1 relativity

  • Drag or tap to launch or drop test balls; tick "Place masses" to add cones instead. Drag a mass to move it.
  • A ball dropped at rest stays at rest: nothing falls. Parallel beam shows paths crossing behind a mass; Flash shows two overlapping wavefronts, which is why you'd see double images.
  • Switching to 2+1 turns light bodies (wedge under 1°) into balls that keep their velocity, so orbits fly apart in straight lines.

Touch

  • Drag empty space: pull back like a slingshot to launch a planet the opposite way. Tap: drop one.
  • Drag a body: grab and throw it. Tap one: select it, then delete it from the panel.
  • Two fingers: pan and pinch to zoom.
